Permissions, users, Disks overview

Read/write/execute in plain English, extra users, and the Disks utility.

Steps

  1. Every file has an owner and permissions: read, write, execute. That is why some folders need sudo.
  2. Settings → Users: add another person a separate account so their files stay private.
  3. Open Disks (gnome-disk-utility): see drives, partitions, and mount points. Useful to check a USB or second SSD.
  4. Do not Format a disk unless you intend to erase it. Double-check the device name.
ls -l
# example line: -rw-rw-r-- 1 bill bill 1234 Sep 10 report.txt

You will learn chmod / chown later if needed. For now: if permission denied, you may be outside your Home or need sudo for a system path.

Disks caution “Format partition” erases data. Prefer Files app for everyday USB copy tasks.
